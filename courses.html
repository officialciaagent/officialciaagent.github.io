Outside Endeavors

Investigating the Latest Status of Spanglish in Multilingual Large Language Models (MLLMs)

This project was conducted through a Research Experience for Undergraduates (REU) program at Portland State University, funded by the National Science Foundation (NSF) under the guidance of Dr. Ameeta Agrawal. The research focused on developing and evaluating mixed-language question answering (QA) systems, with a specific emphasis on Spanglish code-switching. User-generated content such as social media posts, forums, and reviews often blends multiple languages within a single sentence, making it difficult to analyze using traditional methods. To address this, I applied natural language processing (NLP) techniques and leveraged recent advances in large language models (LLMs) to design systems capable of understanding questions in one language and retrieving accurate answers from code-switched or multilingual text.


In addition to evaluating model performance, I explored how prompt design and input structure influence the behavior of multilingual systems when handling code-switched language. Small changes in phrasing or language order often led to noticeably different outputs, highlighting how sensitive these models can be to linguistic context. This reinforced the importance of designing evaluation methods that reflect real-world usage rather than idealized, single-language inputs. Overall, this experience deepened my understanding of how large language models process multilingual data and emphasized the challenges of building systems that can reliably interpret and respond to naturally mixed-language communication.
