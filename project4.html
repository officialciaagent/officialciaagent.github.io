Investigating the Latest Status of Spanglish in Multilingual Large Language Models (MLLMs)


This research project was conducted through a Research Experience for Undergraduates (REU) program at Portland State University's Computer Science Department, funded by the National Science Foundation (NSF) under the mentorship of Dr. Ameeta Agrawal. The project focused on evaluating the performance of multilingual large language models (MLLMs) on code-switched language, with a specific emphasis on Spanglish.

The study involved replicating results from the PANGEA-7B model to validate experimental reliability, followed by a targeted analysis of its ability to handle Spanglish in question answering and translation tasks. Results showed that while the model performed consistently on standard multilingual tasks, it struggled to capture the linguistic complexity and structure of real-world code-switching.

This project highlights the limitations of current multilingual models when applied to hybrid languages and emphasizes the need for improved datasets, evaluation methods, and training strategies. Through this work, I gained experience in natural language processing (NLP), large language model evaluation, dataset handling, and experimental design in multilingual AI systems.
